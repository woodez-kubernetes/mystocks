The Opportunity Score (0–100)

Every tracked stock is assigned an Opportunity Score from 0 to 100. This is the single number you see on the Opportunities page, in holdings tables, and on ticker detail pages. It represents how favorable conditions are for buying that stock right now.

70–100

Strong Buy Signal

Multiple indicators suggest the stock is undervalued or has bullish momentum.
40–69

Hold / Neutral

Mixed signals. The stock is neither clearly overvalued nor undervalued.
0–39

Sell / Caution

Multiple indicators suggest the stock may be overbought or has bearish momentum.
How the Score is Calculated

The Opportunity Score is a weighted average of up to seven sub-scores, each derived from a different technical indicator or data source. Each sub-score is normalized to a 0–100 scale, then combined using these weights:

Indicator Full Weight No Options No Sentiment Technicals Only
RSI (Relative Strength Index)12%15%18%20%
MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)12%15%18%20%
Bollinger Bands12%15%18%20%
SMA (Simple Moving Averages)22%25%22%25%
Volume10%10%12%15%
Sentiment (News)17%20%——
Options Sentiment15%—12%—

Weights adjust automatically based on available data. "Full Weight" applies when sentiment, options, and all technicals are present. SMA always carries the highest weight because trend direction is the strongest predictor of near-term price movement.

The Seven Indicators

RSI — Relative Strength Index (14-day)

What it measures: RSI tracks the speed and magnitude of recent price changes to evaluate whether a stock is overbought or oversold. It oscillates between 0 and 100.

How to read it:

  • RSI below 30 → The stock is oversold. It has been sold off aggressively and may be due for a rebound. This generates a Buy signal.
  • RSI between 30 and 70 → Neutral territory. No strong signal. This generates a Hold signal.
  • RSI above 70 → The stock is overbought. It has run up quickly and may be due for a pullback. This generates a Sell signal.

Sub-score formula: 100 − RSI. A low RSI (oversold) produces a high sub-score, meaning a better buying opportunity.

MACD — Moving Average Convergence Divergence (12/26/9)

What it measures: MACD tracks momentum by comparing two moving averages (12-day fast and 26-day slow). The MACD histogram shows the difference between the MACD line and its 9-day signal line.

How to read it:

  • Histogram positive AND MACD above signal line → Bullish momentum is accelerating. Buy signal.
  • Histogram negative AND MACD below signal line → Bearish momentum. Sell signal.
  • Otherwise → Momentum is transitioning or flat. Hold signal.

Sub-score formula: The histogram is normalized relative to the stock price (histogram / price × 1000), then mapped to a 0–100 scale centered at 50. Positive histogram pushes the score above 50; negative pushes it below.

Bollinger Bands (20-day, 2 std dev)

What it measures: Bollinger Bands create an envelope around the stock price using a 20-day moving average and two standard deviations. When price touches the outer bands, it signals potential reversal.

How to read it:

  • Price near the lower band (within 15% of the range from bottom) → Stock may be oversold and due for a bounce. Buy signal.
  • Price near the upper band (within 15% of the range from top) → Stock may be overbought. Sell signal.
  • Price in the middle → Trading within normal range. Hold signal.

Sub-score formula: (1 − position) × 100, where position is the stock's location within the bands (0 = lower band, 1 = upper band). A price near the lower band produces a high sub-score.

SMA — Simple Moving Averages (50-day & 200-day)

What it measures: SMA smooths out price data to reveal the underlying trend. The 50-day SMA shows the medium-term trend, and the 200-day shows the long-term trend. Their relationship is one of the most widely followed signals in technical analysis.

How to read it:

  • Price above SMA 50 AND SMA 50 above SMA 200 (“Golden Cross”) → Strong uptrend. Buy signal.
  • Price below SMA 50 AND SMA 50 below SMA 200 (“Death Cross”) → Strong downtrend. Sell signal.
  • Mixed → Trend is unclear or transitioning. Hold signal.

Sub-score formula: Starts at 50. Price above SMA 50 adds 15 points; below subtracts 15. SMA 50 above SMA 200 (golden cross) adds 20 points; below (death cross) subtracts 20. Result: range of 15 to 85.

Why this has the highest weight (25%): Trend is the single most important factor. Trading in the direction of the trend gives the highest probability of success.

Volume Ratio (vs 20-day average)

What it measures: Compares today’s trading volume to the 20-day average. Volume confirms price moves — a price move on high volume is more significant than one on low volume.

How to read it:

  • Ratio above 1.5x → Significantly higher volume than normal. Notable activity (institutional buying/selling). Buy signal (high conviction moves).
  • Ratio between 0.5x and 1.5x → Normal volume. Hold signal.
  • Ratio below 0.5x → Very low volume. Price moves lack conviction. Sell signal (weak interest).

Sub-score formula: ratio × 40 + 20. At 1.0x (average volume) the sub-score is 60. Higher volume pushes toward 100; lower volume drops toward 20.

News Sentiment (VADER, 7-day rolling)

What it measures: Headlines from yfinance and RSS feeds (CNBC, MarketWatch, BBC, etc.) are analyzed using the VADER sentiment engine, which scores each headline from −1 (extremely negative) to +1 (extremely positive). The ticker’s sentiment is a 7-day rolling average.

How to read it:

  • Score ≥ 0.15 → Positive news flow. Buy signal.
  • Score between −0.15 and 0.15 → Neutral or mixed news. Hold signal.
  • Score ≤ −0.15 → Negative news flow. Sell signal.

Blended sentiment: Each ticker’s final sentiment blends 70% ticker-specific news (articles mentioning that stock) with 30% overall market sentiment (from RSS feeds). This means even if a specific stock has no recent news, broad market sentiment still factors in.

Sub-score formula: (sentiment + 1) × 50. This maps the −1 to +1 range onto 0–100. A sentiment of 0 (neutral) maps to 50; +1 (very positive) maps to 100; −1 (very negative) maps to 0.

Options Sentiment (Contrarian, 30-day expirations)

What it measures: Options chain data from Yahoo Finance is analyzed across near-term expirations (within 30 days) to gauge how market participants are positioning. This indicator uses a contrarian interpretation — excessive fear (heavy put buying) is treated as a buying opportunity, not a bearish signal.

Three sub-metrics are combined:

1. Put/Call Volume Ratio (40% of options score)

The ratio of total put volume to total call volume across near-term expirations.

  • Ratio above 1.0 → More puts being traded than calls. Excessive fear in the market. Contrarian Buy — fear is often overdone.
  • Ratio between 0.7 and 1.0 → Balanced activity. Neutral.
  • Ratio below 0.7 → Heavy call buying, complacency. Contrarian Sell — the crowd may be too bullish.
2. Put/Call Open Interest Ratio (35% of options score)

Similar to volume ratio but measures accumulated positions rather than daily activity. Open interest changes more slowly and reflects where large/institutional traders have committed capital.

  • High OI ratio (> 1.0) → Heavy put positioning = fear = Contrarian Buy.
  • Low OI ratio (< 0.7) → Heavy call positioning = complacency = Contrarian Sell.
3. Implied Volatility Skew (25% of options score)

Compares the average implied volatility of out-of-the-money (OTM) puts to OTM calls. When put IV is much higher than call IV, it means traders are paying a premium for downside protection.

  • Positive skew (put IV > call IV) → Extreme hedging/fear = Contrarian Buy.
  • Negative skew (call IV > put IV) → Speculative call buying = Contrarian Sell.

Sub-score formula: Weighted average of the three sub-metrics, each mapped to a 0–100 scale. A P/C ratio of 2.0 (extreme fear) maps to ~90; a ratio of 0.3 (extreme complacency) maps to ~10; 1.0 (neutral) maps to 50.

Additional Metrics (displayed but not scored)
  • Max Pain: The strike price where the most options expire worthless. Stock prices tend to gravitate toward max pain near expiration. Shown as a reference point on the ticker detail page.
  • Unusual Activity: Flagged when any single contract has volume exceeding 5x its open interest with at least 100 contracts traded. This can signal large directional bets by informed traders.

Why contrarian? Research shows that extreme put/call ratios are often better used as contrarian indicators. When everyone is buying puts (fear), the selling is often overdone and a bounce follows. When everyone is buying calls (greed), the stock may be overextended.

Note: Not all tickers have options. When options data is unavailable, the other indicators are re-weighted automatically and the options metric is excluded from the composite score.
Price Target Range (Buy & Sell Targets)

What it measures: A computed price range showing potential buy and sell target zones based on all available data — technical indicators, Bollinger Bands, moving averages, options max pain, and the 52-week range.

How it is calculated:

  • Buy Target (support zone): Average of the Bollinger lower band, SMA 200 (if available), options max pain (if below current price), and the 52-week low adjusted toward current price. This represents where the stock is likely to find buying support.
  • Sell Target (resistance zone): Average of the Bollinger upper band, SMA 50 (if above current price), options max pain (if above current price), and the 52-week high adjusted toward current price. This represents where the stock may face selling pressure.

How to read it:

  • If the current price is near or below the buy target, it may be a good entry point.
  • If the current price is near or above the sell target, consider taking profits.
  • The range between buy and sell targets indicates the expected near-term trading range.
Important: Price targets are derived from technical data and represent likely support/resistance zones, not guaranteed price levels. They should be used as one input alongside fundamental analysis and your own research.

The Radar Chart

On the Opportunities page, clicking a stock reveals a radar chart with up to seven axes — one per indicator (Options appears as a 7th axis when data is available). Each axis shows the sub-score (0–100) for that indicator. A large, well-rounded shape means multiple indicators agree the stock is a good opportunity. A lopsided shape means only some indicators are favorable.

Use the radar chart to understand why a stock has its score. For example:

  • A high RSI sub-score but low SMA sub-score might mean the stock is oversold in a downtrend — potentially a value trap.
  • High scores across RSI, BB, and SMA suggest a genuinely undervalued stock in an uptrend — a stronger buy signal.

Important Caveats

  • This is not financial advice. The Opportunity Score is a technical analysis tool, not a recommendation. Always do your own research.
  • Technical indicators look backward. They analyze historical price and volume data. They cannot predict future events like earnings surprises, regulatory changes, or macroeconomic shifts.
  • Sentiment analysis has limitations. VADER analyzes headlines only (not full article content). It can misinterpret sarcasm, context, or domain-specific language.
  • No single indicator is reliable alone. The power of the Opportunity Score comes from combining multiple independent signals. Always look at the radar chart breakdown, not just the final number.
  • Scores change with each refresh. Market data is fetched on-demand when you click "Refresh All." Scores reflect the data at the time of the last refresh.
